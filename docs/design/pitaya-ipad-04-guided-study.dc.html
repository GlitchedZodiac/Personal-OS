THE TEACHING · WRITTEN BEFORE YOU WOKE
Paul answers grief, not curiosity
Paul opens the hardest chapter in the letter by weeping. Before a word about election he wishes himself accursed for his kinsmen — so the argument that follows is not a puzzle about who is chosen. It is an answer to a griever's question: has the word of God failed? Read the chapter with that question in your ear and the furniture rearranges itself.

“But it is not as though the word of God has failed.”

THE WORLD BEHIND THE TEXT
Rome's church was Jewish and Gentile in one room, a generation after most of Israel said no to her Messiah. To a Roman ear that looked like a god who lost — and Paul writes with the synagogue across the street. Chapters 9–11 are one argument, not three; the olive tree of chapter 11 is where the grief of chapter 9 is finally planted.
History, custom, and setting — the room the writer was standing in.

THE DOCTRINE · THE PRACTICE
THE DOCTRINE
The promise never ran to every descendant — it ran through a chosen line inside Israel: Isaac, not Ishmael; Jacob, not Esau. The remnant is not the word failing; the remnant is the shape the word always had.
Calvin — Romans 9 › Henry — Romans 9 ›
Every quotation retrieved from a stored source — never recalled, never invented.
THE PRACTICE
This week, when a verse seems to have failed someone you love, ask first what it actually promised. Precision is a mercy — to them and to the text.
